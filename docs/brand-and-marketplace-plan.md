# The Drove

**Brand and marketplace plan — UK only, online only. No build yet.**

This is the foundation document. It covers the UK online butcher market, the brand, the standard butchers must meet, the commercial model, and the order of work. It is not a product spec and not a build brief.

---

## 1. The short version

**The most popular UK online butcher by scale is Donald Russell.** It won with time, trust signals, consistency and convenience — not with the strictest farming. Around it sit two other kinds of winner: **value players** (The Meat Man, Meat Supermarket) who win on price and next-day volume, and **values players** (Pipers Farm / Pipers & Co, Field & Flower, The Organic Butchery, The Ethical Butcher) who win on story, named farms and taste.

**There is no national consumer brand that is all of these at once:**

- a single trusted shopfront for clean meat
- a published bar (organic, 100% pasture-fed beef and lamb, no routine antibiotics)
- a network of approved UK butchers, not one farm or one cutting room
- online only, UK only
- butchers fulfil and guarantee delivery, so the platform does not take last-mile risk

That is the company to build.

**The brand is The Drove.** A drove is the old British path livestock walked from pasture to market. The modern version is: named farms, through butchers you approve, to the customer’s door. The customer shops one brand. The butchers are the provenance, not a marketplace of random sellers.

**Do not build the website yet.** First lock the name, the standard, founding butchers, and the unit economics. Farmdrop died by scaling logistics and supplier promises before the model was sound. Pipers & Co is already moving into “producers we trust.” The window is to become the **standard**, not another nice farm shop with a URL.

## 2. Market research: who is popular, and why

### 2.1 How big the pond is

- UK **butcher shops** as an industry are about **£3.2bn in 2026**, and have been shrinking about **5% a year**. Shoppers moved to supermarkets, poultry and cheaper proteins. High street independents are under real pressure: more than **1,000 butcheries and slaughterhouses** have closed since 2010. There are still roughly **5,400–5,600** butcher businesses.
- Small abattoirs have collapsed from thousands in the 1970s to a couple of hundred. That matters: the butchers you want (whole-carcass, native breed, organic) depend on remaining local kill capacity.
- **Organic** is growing again. Soil Association figures put the UK organic market at about **£3.9bn**, with supermarket organic unit growth far ahead of non-organic. Riverford has reported a sharp lift in organic meat, including organic chicken up **13%** even at roughly **3×** conventional price. Middle-income families now buy organic most often, even if retirees still spend the most.
- “Grass-fed” is a consumer magnet and a **legal trap**. Under Defra, meat can be called grass-fed if **51%** of the diet is grass. The rest can be grain, soya or biscuit waste. **Pasture for Life** is the only UK mark that means **100% pasture for life**. Organic only requires **60%** forage. If this brand says “grass-fed” without a standard, it will sound like everyone else and eventually get caught.

The customer you want already pays more for meat they trust. They are poorly served by supermarkets (opaque, often imported or grain-finished) and by the high street (fewer shops, uneven organic range). They currently bounce between Waitrose, Abel & Cole, a favourite online butcher, and the occasional farm box.

They have a scatter of specialists.

### 2.3 Why Donald Russell is still the one to beat

If someone in Britain says “I ordered a nice steak online,” they often mean Donald Russell. That is popularity.

They won because they solved **trust and logistics**, not farming:

1. **A seal people already understand** — Royal Warrant, Great Taste stars, 50 years.
2. **The meat arrives the same every time** — frozen at peak, packed like a science. Reviews punish online butchers for warm parcels and missing steaks. Donald Russell made dull reliability a luxury feature.
3. **They sell the occasion** — steak night, Christmas, the fillet for a birthday — then keep the customer with mince, ready meals and a £50 free-delivery threshold.
4. **They can afford to be everywhere** — Vestey capital, a Highland fulfilment centre, SEO, email, TV-adjacent food culture.

Farmison tried to beat them on **breed and story**, doubled in Covid, then could not hold the cost base when demand normalised. Donald Russell bought the name.

**Lesson:** provenance is not enough. The brand that wins this category will feel as safe as Donald Russell and as clean as Pasture for Life.

### 2.4 What customers actually praise (and punish)

Across Trustpilot and specialist reviews, the pattern is stable.

**They praise**

- Taste they can tell from supermarket meat
- Named farm / named animal system
- Arriving cold, on time, well packed
- A human who sorts a problem
- Feeling they supported “proper” farming
- Subscription or freezer boxes so they do not have to think

**They punish**

- Warm or late parcels (this kills brands)
- Missing items
- “Grass-fed” that does not taste like it
- Premium price with supermarket mystery
- Inflexible delivery
- A values story that does not survive a bad courier day

Delivery is not a backend detail. For this category, **delivery is the product.**

### 2.5 The failed model you must not repeat: Farmdrop

Farmdrop (2014–2021) is the cautionary tale for “producers join, we sell to consumers.”

- Promised suppliers **70–75% of retail**.
- Took on **last-mile grocery logistics**.
- Grew to ~450 suppliers. Covid volume. Still unprofitable. Lost on the order of **£10m in a year** even when everyone was buying local online.
- Collapsed two weeks before Christmas. Suppliers had already sent stock. Some had **half their turnover** on the platform. They found out by late-night email.

What suppliers said afterwards: they had given away **the sales relationship**. They never wanted to do that again.

Your model is structurally better **if you keep it**:

- Butchers ship and guarantee delivery (no Farmdrop fleet).
- You are meat-only (no veg-box logistics).
- Approval is a gate, not an open farm directory.
- You are the seller of record, but the butcher keeps their own name on the cut and can still sell locally.

The butcher must never feel they have donated their customers to a startup that might vanish in December.

## 3. The gap (and the trap)

### 3.1 The gap

| Customer need | Who almost does it | What is missing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “I want a steak I can trust tonight” | Donald Russell | Clean farming bar |
| “I want organic meat” | Organic Butchery, Abel & Cole, Riverford | Range, multi-region butchers, one meat-first brand |
| “I want 100% grass-fed” | Primal, Farm2Fork, Ethical Butcher, Macbeth’s | National range, pork/chicken done honestly, brand scale |
| “I want to support small farms” | Field & Flower, Pipers | A hard standard, not a vibe |
| “I am a butcher who cannot afford a proper DTC site” | Shopify + Instagram, or Pipers & Co | A national consumer brand that still lets them remain the butcher |

**The gap in one line:** a Donald Russell-level destination whose only products would be allowed in a Pasture for Life / Soil Association conversation, fulfilled by many butchers, not one factory.

### 3.2 The trap

Pipers & Co is building a warm, family, “producers we know” marketplace. If you launch as “butchers can sign up and sell on our site,” you will look like a thinner Pipers, or like Etsy for meat.

You do not win by being a directory.

You win by being **the brand customers already trust**, with butchers as the supply craft behind it.

Consumer experience: *I shop The Drove.*  
Reality: *An approved butcher in Devon or Yorkshire cut this today and is on the hook for the parcel.*

## 4. Brand

### 4.1 Why not “Clean Butcher”

The working domain `cleanbutcher.co.uk` describes the job. It is not a brand you can build on.

- It sits in a line with **The Ethical Butcher** and **The Organic Butchery**. You become the third lecture, not the first destination.
- “Clean” is American wellness language. In Britain it can sound like a hygiene rating, or like a fad diet.
- It is almost impossible to own. Anyone can say they are a clean butcher.
- It does not give you a world: no ritual, no visual system, no butcher-side poetry. “Join Clean Butcher” is a job ad. “Join the Drove” is a membership.

Keep `cleanbutcher.co.uk` as a redirect if you already own it. Do not print it on a box.

### 4.2 The name: **The Drove**

**A drove** is the route livestock walked from grazing country to market. Drovers moved herds along those roads for centuries. The brand is that path, rebuilt: pasture → approved butcher → doorstep.

**Why this name**

- It is British without being Ye Olde. It belongs to land and trade, not to a founder’s surname and not to a diet.
- It is a **platform metaphor**. A marketplace is a drove. Butchers join a drove. Customers meet the herd at the end of the road.
- It can hold beef, lamb, pork, poultry and game. Pigs and chickens cannot honestly be “100% grass-fed.” They can belong on a drove.
- It is not preachy. Ethical Butcher argues. The Drove assumes.
- It can grow: The Drove Standard, Drove Butchers, The Drove Box, Drove Notes (journal), Drove Kitchen (later). You will not need a rename when you add bone broth or a butcher in Orkney.
- Candidate domains with no public DNS as of this research (confirm before registering): `thedrove.co.uk`, `eatdrove.co.uk`, `wearedrove.co.uk`, `drovemeat.co.uk`. `drove.co.uk` is taken. Run a proper UKIPO search before spending on identity.

**Spoken:** “the drove”  
**URL:** thedrove.co.uk  
**Company:** The Drove Ltd (or Drove Market Ltd if the name search collides)

**Known collisions (manageable, not fatal)**

- A small restaurant in Newhaven called The Drove
- The Highland Drove, a Cumbrian inn
- Ordinary English use of “drove road”

None is a national meat retailer. Still: **UKIPO search + solicitor opinion before a designer starts.** If the name is blocked, use the backup, do not force it.

### 4.3 Backup names (in order)

1. **Well Grazed** — clearest consumer line. British understatement. Domains `wellgrazed.co.uk` / `well-grazed.co.uk` look free. Weaker as a long-term house brand because it is a description. Fine if The Drove fails legal.
2. **Pasture-Led** — the farming term. Ownable as one word. Sits close to Pasture for Life; they could be a partner or an opponent. Use only if you want the brand to *be* the method.
3. Do not use **Fold** — Fold London (fashion), FÔLD (Pilates), Fold Hill Foods (pet food), Fold U.K. Limited (food packaging), `fold.co.uk` taken.
4. Do not use **Grazed** alone — dissolved restaurant company, `grazed.co.uk` already resolves, Sussex Grazed project.

### 4.4 Positioning

**For** households who already care what their meat ate  
**The Drove is** the UK’s approved shop for cleaner meat  
**That** sells organic, pasture-raised, no-routine-antibiotics meat from named British farms  
**Unlike** Donald Russell (premium, not strict), Pipers & Co (broad food, soft standard), or a single farm box (narrow range)  
**The Drove** is one brand, many butchers, one published bar.

**Category we choose to own:** *Approved clean meat, delivered.*  
Not: online butcher directory. Not: regenerative media brand. Not: cheap bulk meat.

### 4.5 Brand idea

**Meat should travel a known path.**

Supermarket meat has no path. It has a label. The Drove makes the path visible: the field, the butcher, the pack, the door. If any step is unknown, it does not ship.

### 4.6 Only one promise

> **Clean British meat, from butchers we have actually approved.**

Everything else is proof of that sentence.

### 4.7 Messaging house

**Masterline**  
The Drove. From pasture, through butchers we trust, to your door.

**Customer lines** (pick one for the site, keep the rest for campaigns)

- Meat as it should be.
- Named farms. Approved butchers. UK only.
- If we cannot tell you how it was raised, we will not sell it.
- Organic. Pasture-fed. No routine antibiotics.
- The modern drove.

**Butcher lines**

- Join the Drove.
- You cut. You ship. We bring the households.
- A national shop window. You keep the craft.
- We sell at retail. You are paid at wholesale. No shop rent, no ads team.

**Words we use**  
pasture, named, approved, British, whole carcass, slow-grown, outdoor, traceable, butcher, farm, doorstep, winter forage, native, organic, guaranteed.

**Words we do not use**  
clean eating, keto, primal, superfood, carbon negative (unless independently measured), ethical (as a badge), artisan (unless a butcher earns it), grass-fed (unless it is 100% and evidenced), gourmet, finest, passion, journey, disrupt.

**Tone**  
Calm, specific, adult. Like a good butcher who has time to explain, not a campaigner and not a luxury catalogue. Short sentences. Name the farm. Name the breed. Name the hanging days. Never say “we care” — show the rule.

### 4.8 Personality

| We are | We are not |
| --- | --- |
| A standard | A directory |
| Pastoral and precise | Rustic-cliché or steakhouse-black |
| Firm about farming | Smug about customers |
| Warm to butchers | Soft on quality |
| UK and proud | Nationalist or tartan-kitsch |
| Quietly sure | Shouty “revolution” |

**Brand film in one shot:** a drove road in rain, cattle on a mixed sward, a butcher’s ticket, a labelled parcel on a British doorstep. No slow-mo knives. No founders in a warehouse.

### 4.9 Visual system (for a designer, later)

**Direction:** cartographic pastoral. The identity comes from **routes and land**, not from butcher stripes.

- **Colour:** chalk / warm paper, deep pasture green, iron, a single oxblood for meat and sale states. No bright grass-green, no gold foil luxury.
- **Type:** one good British serif for the wordmark and headlines (think the register of a OS map or an old stock catalogue, recut tightly). One unfussy grotesque for UI and packing labels. No scripts, no blackletter.
- **Mark:** a path, not an animal. A simplified drove road / fold in the land. Must work at 16px on a favicon and on a waxed box.
- **Photography:** working farms in weather; animals that look like animals; butchers’ hands and tickets; cardboard, wool, paper — not marble boards and rosemary clichés. Every product image later must be **that butcher’s actual cut**, not stock.
- **Graphic device:** a faint map grain or contour, used on tissue, tape, and the butcher card in the box.
- **Packaging:** The Drove is the outer brand. Inside: a card with butcher, farm, breed, kill date or batch, cooking note. The customer should be able to photograph the card. That card is marketing.

Avoid the three clichés of this category: black-and-gold steak club, hessian farm shop, and wellness-studio sage.

### 4.10 Brand architecture

```
THE DROVE                 consumer brand, seller of record
  └ The Drove Standard    the published farming and butchery bar
  └ Drove Butchers        approved members (named, never generic)
  └ The Drove Box         subscription / butcher’s selection
  └ Drove Notes           journal: farms, cuts, recipes, standards
```

Butchers keep their trading name. On site: **“Swaledale-style attribution”** — *The Drove ribeye · cut by [Butcher] · from [Farm], [county] · 100% pasture-fed, 28-day dry-aged.*

The customer is buying The Drove. The butcher is credited like a maker, not listed like a third-party seller.

### 4.11 Proof, not posture

Trust in this market is borrowed from **seals people already know**. Use them. Do not invent a fake certification.

On pack and PDP, in this order:

1. Soil Association organic (where true)
2. Pasture for Life (where true)
3. The Drove Standard mark (your bar, clearly “our rule,” never implied as independent)
4. Named farm and county
5. Named butcher
6. Hygiene rating of the cutting site (link)
7. UK-reared, UK-cut

If a product has none of 1 or 2, it must still pass 3, and the page must say **exactly** why.

## 5. The Drove Standard (the real product)

This is the brand. If the standard is soft, the name is decoration.

Publish it in plain English. Make it harder to join than Shopify. Make it easier to understand than organic law.

### 5.1 Non-negotiable, all species

- **UK-reared and UK-cut.** No imported meat, no “packed in the UK.”
- **Named farm** (or named producer group with a public list).
- **Named butcher** with a live cutting site.
- **No routine antibiotics.** Sick animals may be treated. Those animals follow organic-style withdrawal and, where organic, organic rules. Prophylactic mass medication is out.
- **No cages, no farrowing crates, no zero-graze dairy-style beef systems.**
- **Full traceability** farm → abattoir → cutting plant → parcel.
- **Stunning** before slaughter. State it. The Organic Butchery already found this matters to their customers.
- **Cutting site:** registered food business, typically FHRS **4 or 5**, relevant FSA approval where they cut POAO, product liability insurance, recall process.
- **Delivery:** butcher guarantees temperature-controlled arrival. The Drove sets the SLA; the butcher owns the courier relationship and the failure.

### 5.2 By species (honest language)

| Species | Required | How we say it | Never say |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Beef** | 100% pasture-fed for life (Pasture for Life or equivalent documented diet). Organic preferred. Native or traditional breeds preferred, not required. Dry-aged on the bone for a published minimum (recommend 21–28 days). | 100% pasture-fed. Organic where certified. | “Grass-fed” alone |
| **Lamb / hogget / mutton** | Same pasture rule. Seasonal. | 100% pasture-fed. Name the season. | Year-round “spring lamb” |
| **Pork** | Outdoor-bred and outdoor-reared. Organic **or** a published high-welfare pasture system. No routine antibiotics. No crates. Soy in feed: disclose; organic soy or certified responsible; prefer soy-free. | Outdoor-reared. Organic where certified. Pasture-raised. | Grass-fed pork |
| **Chicken / turkey / goose** | Organic **or** slow-grown, genuine pasture access (not a pop-hole on a shed). No routine antibiotics. Name the growth days. | Organic / slow-grown, pasture-raised. | Grass-fed chicken |
| **Game** | UK wild, legally taken, from a named estate or game dealer. | Wild. Named estate. | Farmed as wild |

If a butcher cannot meet beef/lamb pasture rules, they can still join for pork, poultry or game. The catalogue must never mix a grain-finished steak into the beef aisle.

### 5.3 Claims discipline

Legal and brand risk live here.

- **Organic** — only with a current certificate, logo rules followed, and the certifier named.
- **100% pasture-fed** — diet evidence, ideally Pasture for Life. Keep records.
- **No routine antibiotics** — written farm policy, not a vibe.
- **Grass-fed** — avoid as a headline. If used, only with “100%” and the evidence behind it.
- Nutrition / health — no medical claims. Omega-3 differences can be discussed with citations, later, in Drove Notes. Not on the product button.

### 5.4 What we will not sell (at launch)

- Commodity supermarket-style chicken
- Hormone or growth-promoter systems (already illegal in UK — still say we refuse imported equivalents)
- Wet-aged anonymous beef
- Mixed-origin mince
- Butcher’s own-label products that fail the standard even if the rest of their counter is beautiful
- Anything the butcher cannot ship to the customer’s postcode inside the SLA

A **narrow, boringly consistent** catalogue will beat a wide, slightly dodgy one.

## 6. Who it is for

### 6.1 Customer (in order)

1. **The organic household** — already on Abel & Cole, Riverford, Waitrose organic, or a farm shop. Meat is the disappointing part of their shop. Age ~32–55, ABC1, London, Bristol, Brighton, Manchester, Edinburgh, Oxford, Bath, Leeds. Will pay 1.5–3× Tesco for a story they believe **and** a parcel that arrives cold.
2. **The health-motivated cook** — antibiotics, ultra-processed, “what did this animal eat.” Overlaps with Primal / Ethical Butcher. Do not become a keto shop. Speak in farming facts, not macros.
3. **The Sunday-roast traditionalist** — wants a butcher, cannot find a good one locally. Donald Russell’s customer, if you can match reliability and beat provenance.
4. **Not for launch:** bargain bulk, bodybuilding volumes (MuscleFood), restaurants (maybe later), export, Northern Ireland complexity until mainland is smooth.

### 6.2 Butcher (in order)

1. **Whole-carcass independents** who already cut organic or Pasture for Life livestock and hate Instagram as a sales channel.
2. **Farm butchers** with a killing/cutting arrangement and spare wholesale capacity.
3. **Respected regional names** who want national reach without building Farmison.

**Not:** a high-street shop selling mostly British conventional from a wholesaler, even if they are charming.

Founding set (target **6–10**, not 50):

- At least two beef/lamb butchers (e.g. West Country + North or Scotland)
- One pork specialist
- One poultry farm/butcher
- Geographic spread so Christmas and spring lamb are not one county’s weather
- Every founder able to fulfil **a full household box**, not only a novelty steak

You are recruiting a **cast**, not opening a floodgate.

## 7. Commercial model

### 7.1 How money moves

You are **merchant of record**. Not an agent. Not eBay.

1. Customer pays The Drove retail.
2. The Drove pays the butcher a contracted **wholesale price**.
3. The Drove keeps the spread.
4. The butcher packs, ships, and **guarantees** delivery to the SLA.
5. Delivery fee: charged to the customer at checkout (cleanest), or built into retail (simpler feeling, messier maths). Recommend **visible delivery, free over a threshold** (Donald Russell uses £50; organic baskets will sit higher — test **£75–£100**).

The butcher’s wholesale is not “70% of whatever we feel like.” It is a **price list**: SKU, spec, pack weight, wholesale, recommended retail, min order.

### 7.2 Illustrative economics (planning only)

Example: 28-day pasture-fed ribeye, 2 × 227g.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Customer pays (ex-delivery) | £22.00 |
| Butcher wholesale | £14.30 (65%) |
| Payment + ops (cards, support, software) | £1.50 (7%) |
| Contribution to brand, ads, waste, refunds | £6.20 (28%) |

**65% to the butcher** is the planning default. Farmdrop’s 70–75% plus **their** last mile was a suicide pact. You can pay ~65% because you are **not** collecting, warehousing or delivering.

If a butcher’s own site already sells that steak at £22 and they keep ~£20 after fees, The Drove looks like a pay cut **unless** you sell it as:

- customers they would never have acquired
- no ads, no site, no CS evenings
- whole-carcass balancing (you can feature mince and shin, not only fillet)
- a brand that makes their farming **legible**

Founding butchers need **honest volume**. Empty promises are how Farmdrop is remembered.

**Do not launch with 30% take and a smile.** Model three numbers before a single landing page:

- Wholesale the butcher will accept
- Retail the customer will pay twice
- Contribution after refunds and failed deliveries (plan **5–8%** of GMV for things going wrong; chilled food is unforgiving)

### 7.3 Who pays when a parcel fails

Write this into the butcher contract before you write a homepage.

- **Courier failure / warm on arrival / missing item:** butcher makes it right (reship or refund via you). Their guarantee.
- **The Drove overselling / site error / duplicate charge:** you make it right.
- **Farming claim found false:** immediate delist, you refund the customer, you recover from the butcher, you publish a note. One lie poisons the standard.

The customer only ever contacts **The Drove**. That is the cost of being the brand.

### 7.4 Split baskets (design this commercially now)

If someone buys Devon beef and Yorkshire chicken, two vans will roll. That feels cheap.

**Launch rule:** allow multi-butcher baskets, but:

- Show **each** delivery and each fee before pay
- Default merchandising toward **one-butcher boxes** and “this butcher can fulfil your whole shop”
- Subscription boxes are **one butcher per drop**, rotating monthly if you want variety

Phase 1 can even be **one butcher per order**. That is operationally calmer and still a network on the site (shop by butcher, or a shared catalogue that routes to one).

Recommendation: **shared catalogue, smart routing, one-butcher-first.** Do not hide multiple parcels.

### 7.5 Pricing stance

Premium, not luxury-theatre. Sit with Organic Butchery / Field & Flower, not with Meat Man, not with Hawksmoor at home.

- Everyday: mince, stewing, chicken — priced so a household can actually switch
- Pride cuts: steaks, legs, birds — priced as the treat they are
- Never discount the standard. Discount **the cut** (shoulder vs loin), or a first-box offer, not “20% off organic.”

## 8. How it works (operations, still no build)

### 8.1 Customer path

1. Arrive for the brand (Christmas, Sunday, “organic meat delivery”).
2. Believe the standard in **one screen**.
3. Shop cuts **or** a box. See farm + butcher on every tile.
4. Delivery date from the butcher who will ship. Postcode checked.
5. Pay The Drove.
6. Get one tracking thread per parcel, branded as The Drove, even if the courier is DPD.
7. Open a Drove box with a butcher card.
8. If something is wrong, one CS channel. You chase the butcher.

### 8.2 Butcher path

1. Apply (not “sign up and go live”).
2. Submit certificates, farm list, hygiene, insurance, sample spec, courier plan, wholesale list.
3. You mystery-shop a paid sample order to a real address.
4. Contract: wholesale, SLA, claims, brand use, data, termination.
5. Onboarding: photo rules, pack spec, branded tape/cards you supply, how orders arrive (email + portal — later).
6. Live, but **pending** on the public site until first 20 orders clear without a temperature complaint.
7. Quarterly review. Fail the standard, leave the drove.

“Pending approval” is not a toggle. It is a **probation**.

### 8.3 What you owe the butcher every week

- Forecast, especially for Christmas (this category **lives or dies in December**)
- Cut mix so they are not left with 40 rumps
- Payment on a boring rhythm (weekly, not “after we’ve got cashflow”)
- A human buyer, not only a portal

If you cannot name who that buyer is, you are not ready to recruit.

### 8.4 Pack and cold chain (the standard, not the app)

Specify now, because it is the brand in physical form:

- Insulated liner, adequate ice sheets for **48 hours**
- Outer branded The Drove
- Inner labels: legal name, allergens, storage, use-by, farm, butcher, organic/PfL codes
- No butcher using supermarket bags “just this once”
- Couriers: only services with a chilled or proven overnight meat record (the specialists use next-day, order-cut-off late morning)
- Remote postcodes: be honest. Highlands and Islands may wait or pay more. Do not promise Donald Russell coverage on day one.

### 8.5 Legal shape (planning, not advice)

Take proper advice. Budget for it. This is a food business pretending to be a website if you skip it.

- Register as a **food business operator** with the local authority **≥28 days** before trade, including the **office** even if you never touch a carcass.
- Distance selling: Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013, e-commerce regulations, allergens **before pay and on delivery**.
- Perishable goods have limited cancellation rights — say so clearly.
- Labelling and composition law on every SKU.
- If you never cut meat, you may not need a cutting-plant approval; **each butcher might**. You still need traceability, recall, and a hygiene system for the operation you do control (site content, supplier approval, complaint handling).
- VAT, butcher contracts, data (you hold the customer; be careful what you pass to butchers), insurance (product + professional + cyber).
- Advertising: ASA will treat “grass-fed” and “no antibiotics” as claims that need evidence.

Merchant of record means **you** are the seller in the customer’s eyes and in a lot of law. That is correct for the brand. It is also why approval cannot be light.

## 9. Go to market (brand first)

### 9.1 Do not buy ads until the box is right

The first 200 customers should come from:

1. The founding butchers’ existing loyalists (offer them a Drove SKU they cannot get locally — a box, a mixed selection)
2. Farm and Soil Association / Pasture for Life adjacency (events, newsletters, not logo-slapping)
3. A simple waitlist story: *the approved path for cleaner meat*
4. Food writers who already cover Field & Flower and organic boxes
5. Christmas  — only if you are operationally ready **by October**. If not, skip a Christmas rather than become Farmdrop.

### 9.2 Launch narrative

**Public:** “The Drove is the UK shop for meat that meets a hard standard: organic or 100% pasture-fed, no routine antibiotics, cut by butchers we have approved. They ship. They guarantee it. We stand behind it.”

**Not:** “A marketplace where butchers can sign up.”

Press should meet **one farm, one butcher, one standard page.** Not a founder origin myth unless it is short and true.

### 9.3 Retention (this is the business)

Donald Russell and Field & Flower are subscription-and-habit machines.

- **The Drove Box** monthly or fortnightly, one butcher, whole-carcass mix, freeze-friendly
- Cook notes that teach shin as well as sirloin
- A freeze-first pack so midweek works
- Second order incentive, not a forever coupon

The brand becomes a household staple or it becomes a Christmas toy.

### 9.4 What you will be asked in every interview

Have one sentence each:

- What does grass-fed mean here? → *We do not use the 51% legal minimum. Beef and lamb are 100% pasture-fed.*
- Are you a marketplace? → *We are the seller. Butchers are approved makers who fulfil.*
- Why not just buy from a farm? → *You can. We exist so you do not need ten websites and a research degree.*
- Is meat bad for the climate? → *Industrial meat is a different product. We sell animals that ate pasture and were not routinely drugged. Eat less, eat better, know the path.* Stay out of culture-war branding.

## 13. Open decisions (do not hide these)

1. **The Drove vs Well Grazed** — brand-first vs instantly readable. Recommendation: The Drove, if UKIPO is clean.
2. **65% wholesale default** — must be tested with real butchers. Some organic chicken will not bear it; some aged beef will.
3. **Fresh vs frozen** — Field & Flower/Swaledale win on fresh; Donald Russell/Pipers win on freezer. Recommendation: **fresh with freeze-on-arrival instructions**, frozen SKUs allowed if labelled and never passed off as fresh.
4. **One butcher per order at launch** — operationally wiser. Slightly less “platform.” Recommendation: yes.
5. **Organic required, or organic-or-Pasture-for-Life?** — Recommendation: **either**, with the badge shown; never neither for ruminants.
6. **Halal** — Organic Butchery made “always stunned, no halal” a pillar. Decide explicitly. Recommendation for a national clean brand: **always stunned**, do not make religion a marketing wedge unless you have a reason.
7. **Operating name vs legal entity** — keep them aligned if you can.

## 14. Competitive posture (one glance)

**Donald Russell** — the incumbent of convenience and occasion. Respect their logistics. Do not copy their farming vagueness.

**Pipers & Co** — the incumbent of trust-as-family. They will be compared in every article. Your counter is a **harder bar** and **butcher identity**, not another pantry.

**Field & Flower** — the incumbent of the weekly box. Beat them on farming strictness; match them on human CS.

**Ethical Butcher / Organic Butchery / Primal** — proof of demand. Recruit from the same farms; do not pick a fight in public.

**Meat Man** — ignore.

**Farmison** — a ghost. Story without operations is a sale to Vestey.

## 15. What “brand first” means in practice

Until Phase 0 is done, the company is these artefacts and nothing else:

1. A name you legally own  
2. A standard a farmer can fail  
3. A voice that does not sound like a directory  
4. A box a person would keep on the table  
5. Six butchers who would be proud to put their names inside it  
6. A margin that survives a failed DPD van  

That is enough to build on. The website is the last expression of it, not the first.
